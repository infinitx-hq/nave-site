AI GEN GUIDANCE

Two tools your agents load before generating any visual: the meta prompt that turns a rough idea into a production-ready image prompt, and the camera language that makes generated video move like cinema — with a visual reference for every movement family.

TOOL 01

THE META PROMPT FOR AI IMAGES

Don't prompt the image model directly. Give this to your agent (or any LLM) with your idea in [INSERT IDEA] — it does the visual-director thinking and hands you one refined, production-ready prompt.

META-PROMPT.TXT
You are an expert visual director, cinematographer, and AI prompt engineer.

Your task is to transform a basic idea into a highly detailed, visually striking, and coherent AI image prompt.

Input: [INSERT IDEA]

Follow these steps:

1. Clarify the core subject and intent
- What is happening?
- What is the emotional tone?

2. Define the visual composition
- Camera angle (wide, close-up, top-down, etc.)
- Framing (centered, rule of thirds, negative space)
- Depth (foreground, midground, background)

3. Define lighting
- Type (soft, harsh, golden hour, cinematic, volumetric)
- Direction (backlit, side-lit, overhead)
- Mood (ethereal, dramatic, natural, moody)

4. Define environment and setting
- Location and textures
- Atmosphere (fog, dust, particles, air quality)

5. Define subject details
- Pose, expression, gesture
- Clothing, materials, physical details

6. Define visual style
- Photography, cinematic, painting, 3D, surreal, etc.
- Influences (brands, artists, film styles if relevant)

7. Define color palette
- Dominant tones
- Contrast level
- Color harmony (muted, vibrant, monochrome)

8. Define technical quality
- Lens (35mm, 85mm, macro, etc.)
- Resolution and detail (ultra-detailed, 8k, film grain, etc.)

9. Add subtle enhancement elements
- Motion cues, particles, reflections, depth effects

10. Output a single refined prompt
- Clean, flowing, no bullet points
- No explanations

Make the result visually precise, emotionally aligned, and production-ready.

Avoid clichés and generic phrases.

TOOL 02

CAMERA MOVEMENT LANGUAGE

Camera movement is how a shot tells the story — it sets mood and steers the eye. When you prompt AI video (or brief your agent), name the movement with this vocabulary. Each family below has a reference frame showing the feel it creates.

01

Static Shots

  • Static: camera remains completely still — calm, observational scenes.
  • Locked Down: camera fixed in position — dialogue or focused action.
02

Horizontal Movements

  • Pan: camera swivels left/right from a fixed position — reveals or follows action.
  • Sweeping Pan: smooth, slow pan across a landscape — adds grandeur.
  • Whip Pan: fast, energetic pan — transitions or action beats.
  • Tracking Shot: camera moves alongside the subject, following smoothly.
  • Dynamic Tracking: fast-paced tracking — high-energy or chase scenes.
03

Vertical Movements

  • Tilt: camera moves up/down from a fixed position — reveals vertical elements.
  • Pedestal: camera moves vertically while staying level — emphasizes height.
  • Epic Crane Shot: dramatic rise or descent — grand, epic feel.
  • Jib Shot: smaller-scale vertical move — adds fluidity.
04

Depth-Based Movements

  • Zoom: lens magnifies or reduces without camera movement — draws focus.
  • Dramatic Zoom: quick zoom-in on a reaction or critical detail.
  • Cinematic Push-In: smooth forward move toward the subject — emotional intensity.
  • Pull Out: camera moves backward — reveals context, creates distance.
  • Dolly Shot: camera physically moves toward/away — depth and tension.
  • Dolly Zoom: dolly in + zoom out (or reverse) — surreal, warping effect.
05

Circular / Rotational Movements

  • Arc: camera curves around the subject — highlights action or emotion.
  • 360° Orbit: full circle around the subject — dynamic perspective.
  • Roll: camera rotates on its own axis — disorienting, dramatic.
  • Dutch Angle: tilted diagonal frame — tension or unease.
06

Aerial Movements

  • Aerial Glide: smooth movement from above — bird's-eye view.
  • Drone Shot: sweeping high-altitude perspectives of landscapes or cities.
07

Handheld / Immersive Movements

  • Handheld: raw, intimate, or chaotic feel.
  • Shaky Cam: deliberate instability — intensity and realism in action.
  • Body Cam: camera on the actor — subjective, immersive.
  • Steadicam: stabilized movement — smooth tracking without the shake.
08

Complex / Dramatic Shots

  • Hero Shot: camera angles up at the subject — powerful, larger-than-life.
  • Over-the-Shoulder: looks over a character's shoulder — their perspective.
  • Crash Zoom: sudden fast zoom — action or dramatic moments.
  • Parallax Shift: background/foreground separation — adds depth.
  • Push and Pan: forward movement + panning — complexity and dynamism.
  • Snap Zoom: quick zoom in/out — highlights a key moment.
09

Time-Related Movements

  • Time-Lapse: passage of time via sped-up frames.
  • Slow Motion: slows the action — emphasis on detail and emotion.
  • Hyperlapse: time-lapse where the camera also travels through space.
10

Perspective / Character-Based Movements

  • Point of View (POV): the character's direct perspective — full immersion.
  • Subjective Camera: follows one character's experience — empathy or suspense.
  • Overhead Shot: directly above the subject — dramatic, revealing.
  • Reverse Tracking: camera moves backward keeping the subject in focus.
How to use this in your pipeline: run your idea through the meta prompt first, then name ONE movement from this page in your video prompt (e.g. "cinematic push-in", "360° orbit", "true top-down drone view"). One clear movement per shot beats five stacked ones.
